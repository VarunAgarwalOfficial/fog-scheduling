Fog scheduling

Users send tasks to a nearby small computer, a fog node, which keeps a few and passes the rest to a large but distant cloud; a scheduling policy decides which tasks stay and which run first. Press Play to watch the Priority policy handle one workload, one tick (one step of time) after another.

Six policies on the same tasks: how long each makes tasks wait, and what it costs

Each policy was run in full, without animation, on the same tasks over many workloads (one per seed, a number that fixes the random tasks). Bars are means; the whiskers show the 95% confidence interval, the range the true mean very likely falls in.

    Who waits: response time by priority
      Against queueing theory: one queue

      Take the network away except for one fog node that keeps every task: a single server, 50 users, work of 1 to 9 ticks. Each policy here is only its order; nobody is sent away. For first come, first served the mean wait on this queue has an exact formula (the discrete-time form of the Pollaczek-Khinchine formula). The dashed line is that formula; the dots are the simulation with 95% intervals.
